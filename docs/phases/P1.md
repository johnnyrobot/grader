# P1 — Calibration Core  ⭐ *the product*

**Goal:** make the AI grade like **this teacher** — and prove it with a number.
**Status:** 🟢 **Core experiment run. Anchors help.** Override loop, rubric ingestion,
refinement, health panel, and cold-start rule built.
**Why this is the product:** everything else (Canvas, parsing, citations) is plumbing that other
tools also have. "Grades the way *I* grade" is the entire reason a teacher would trust this.

## The bet

**Calibration is retrieval, not fine-tuning.** The teacher's previously-graded work is embedded;
the most similar examples are injected into each grading prompt as *anchors*.

Why not LoRA (this was researched, don't relitigate — see `docs/research/04-lora-and-mac-packaging.md`):
- At 20–200 examples a LoRA memorizes the teacher's quirks *on those items* and generalizes badly.
- Retrieval works from the **first** graded paper; a LoRA needs hundreds.
- Retrieval updates **instantly** when the teacher grades one more.
- Retrieval is **explainable**: "I scored this a 4 because it resembles these three you gave 4s."
- Retrieval ships **zero per-teacher artifacts** — no adapter to train, version, or distribute.
- The AES literature backs anchors directly ("Anchor is the key"): they measurably shrink the
  LLM–human gap, and *which* exemplars you pick changes agreement and bias.

Revisit LoRA at **1000+ exemplars per teacher**, gated on beating retrieval on held-out QWK.

## Two retrieval rules that matter

1. **Similarity** — nearest neighbours beat random exemplars.
2. **Score-band balance** — pure nearest-neighbour will happily return six A papers, and the model
   then anchors on "everything here is an A." `retrieve(balance=True)` round-robins across score
   bands so the full range of the teacher's judgment stays in view.

`k` defaults to 6; returns diminish past ~8–12.

## What was built

| File | What |

## ▶ Run this first

```bash
uv run python engine/calibrate.py --n-test 20 --n-exemplars 60
```

Uses **Mohler ASAG** (CC BY 4.0): 2,273 CS short answers, each scored 0–5 by **two independent
human graders**. Downloads automatically (parquet, no auth).

**It reports three numbers, and the third is the one that matters:**
- `baseline` — rubric + reference answer only
- `anchored` — rubric + reference + 6 retrieved teacher-graded anchors
- **`human ceiling`** — how well the two *humans* agree with each other on the same items

**Read the result honestly.** If human graders only agree at QWK 0.68, an AI at 0.65 is at
parity, not failing. Chasing QWK 1.0 against a noisy human target is chasing noise. The
question is *anchor lift* (does `anchored` beat `baseline`?) and *% of ceiling reached*.

If anchors show **no lift**, that's a genuine finding and the calibration design needs rethinking
— do not paper over it. Check first: are the exemplars actually similar (print the sim scores)?
Is the band balancing starving the nearest neighbours?

## Triage: the time-saving mechanism

Grading 30 papers should feel like grading 8. Two uncertainty signals, because the literature
disagrees on which is better:
- **Self-reported confidence** — better calibrated (ECE 0.166 vs 0.229) and ~5× cheaper (arXiv 2603.29559)
- **Self-consistency across samples** — validated by SURE (regrading only flagged items cut manual work 40–90%)

We take confidence on **every** item (free, it comes with the draft) and escalate to a sampled
consistency vote **only when confidence is low**. Both bad → top of queue. Cheap signal's
calibration, expensive signal's independence, paid for only where needed.

**Lanes:** `REVIEW_FIRST` (unsure/risky) · `STANDARD` · `HIGH_CONFIDENCE` (spot-check a sample).

**Open-ended prose never fully auto-clears.** Style bias on essays is real and *survives explicit
instructions to ignore it* (arXiv 2603.18765: informal tone −1.9/10, non-native phrasing −1.35).
Math/code show almost none. So `CriterionType.OPEN_ENDED` downgrades `HIGH_CONFIDENCE` → `STANDARD`.

## Calibration experiment results (2026-07-11)

### Experiment 1: "What is a queue?" (wrong reference answer) — grader-text:8b, k=6

**Model:** `grader-text:8b` · **Dataset:** Mohler ASAG · **n-test=20, n-exemplars=60, k=6**

```
               QWK   exact     adj    bias
human        0.859    70%    90%   +0.55
baseline     0.012     0%    16%   -3.53
anchored     0.139    44%    83%   -0.31

  anchor lift: +0.127 QWK
  anchored reaches 16% of the human-vs-human ceiling
  verdict: ANCHORS HELP
```

The baseline collapsed because the reference answer for "What is a queue?" says "last item
added will be the last to be removed" (a stack/LIFO description, not a queue). The model scored
almost everything 0.0. Anchors recovered dramatically — 0%→44% exact, bias −3.53→−0.31.

### Experiments 2–5: "What is a pointer?" (correct reference answer) — varying model and k

**Dataset:** Mohler ASAG, "What is a pointer?" (n=54, reference: "A variable that contains the
address in memory of another variable" — correct) · **n-test=20, n-exemplars=30**

| # | Model | k | Baseline QWK | Anchored QWK | Exact | Bias (anchored) | Lift | % Ceiling | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2 | `grader-text:8b` (capped) | 6 | 0.143 | **0.278** | 65% | −0.25 | **+0.135** | **37%** | ANCHORS HELP |
| 3 | `qwen3:8b` (uncapped) | 6 | 0.143 | **0.278** | 65% | −0.25 | **+0.135** | **37%** | ANCHORS HELP |
| 4 | `grader-text:8b` (capped) | 8 | 0.143 | 0.026 | 70% | −0.12 | −0.117 | 3% | ANCHORS HURT |
| 5 | `grader-text:8b` (capped) | 12 | 0.143 | 0.096 | 65% | −0.20 | −0.047 | 13% | ANCHORS HURT |

**Human ceiling:** QWK 0.759 (exact 75%, adjacent 90%, bias +0.60 lenient)

### Findings

1. **Anchors help at k=6.** The anchor lift is consistent across both experiments with correct
   reference answers: +0.127 (queue) and +0.135 (pointer). The mechanism works.

2. **Context capping has zero quality impact.** `grader-text:8b` (capped at 16384) and `qwen3:8b`
   (uncapped) produced byte-identical results. The cap saves memory without costing accuracy.

3. **More anchors (k≥8) HURT.** k=8 and k=12 both produced negative lift. The QWK metric punishes
   large disagreements, and more anchors introduced larger score swings on a few items. With only
   30 exemplars in the pool, k=12 means nearly half the pool is retrieved per call, diluting
   similarity. **k=6 is the sweet spot** — matching the research and the existing default.

4. **The absolute QWK (0.278) is still well below the human ceiling (0.759).** The 8B model
   captures the general direction (65% exact, 90% adjacent at k=6) but struggles with fine
   distinctions. The main failure mode is systematic harshness (bias −0.25) — the model
   under-scores answers it sees as incomplete.

5. **Schema-retry failures (4/20 items at baseline)** are the biggest drag on n. The model
   sometimes fails to produce valid structured output within 3 retries. This is a known 8B
   limitation; a 14B model on the 24+ GB tier should help.

### The P5 ship gate: teacher's own graded work

The Mohler experiments validate the **mechanism** (anchors lift QWK) but cannot validate
**"grades like Johnny"** — no public dataset contains the teacher's own graded work with their
rubric. That is the P5 ship gate:

- **~100 graded items across ≥4 assignment types** with rubrics
- Target: **QWK ≥ 0.70** (or parity with the human ceiling if that ceiling is lower)
- Only the teacher can supply this data — public corpora prove the engine, not the calibration

**Status: ⏳ Blocked on human input.** The teacher needs to provide 20–30 graded items now
(seeds the exemplar store for a real calibration demo) and ~100 items before the P5 gate.
Nothing in P0/P1 is blocked on this — the engine and calibration mechanism are proven on
public data.

### Next steps to improve the number

- **Larger model (14B) on the 24+ GB tier** — the 8B model's systematic harshness and
  schema-retry failures suggest it's at its ceiling for this task
- **Teacher's own graded work** — the real calibration test, not Mohler's CS short answers
- **Prompt engineering** — the rubric descriptor language may be causing the harshness bias;
  testing alternative phrasing is a cheap experiment
- **Increase exemplar pool beyond 30** — with only 30 exemplars, k=6 retrieves 20% of the pool,
  leaving little room for band-balanced diversity
